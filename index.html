Burin Naowarat

burin.naowarat@ed.ac.uk

I wonder how neural networks process complex sensory input to make accurate predictions. I am currently exploring this question through interpretability research on speech models, studying geometric properties of hidden structures in their representations.

I’m a PhD student at the Centre for Speech Technology Research (CSTR) in the School of Informatics at the University of Edinburgh, supervised by Sharon Goldwater and Hao Tang. I started in September 2024 and expect to finish in April 2028. My current work studies how neural models represent word timing and how much we can learn about a concept by examining a low-dimensional subspace of the model’s representations.

Before starting my PhD, I completed a research master’s degree focused on automatic speech recognition (ASR) at Chulalongkorn University, supervised by Ekapol Chuangsuwanich. During my master’s, I was an applied scientist intern with Amazon Alexa in Cambridge, UK. I later worked as a language modelling engineer on in-car ASR systems at Cerence.

Selected research

Deep Speech Representations Track Word Timing along a Low-Dimensional Circular Trajectory

Burin Naowarat, Hao Tang, and Sharon Goldwater.

SLT, 2026. Paper ↗ Code ↗

We found that self-supervised, supervised, and codec speech models represent word timing along circular trajectories. Using the end of each rotation in HuBERT’s representations to detect word boundaries, we achieved a boundary F-score of 64.3% on LibriSpeech.

A framework for analyzing concept representations in neural models

Burin Naowarat, Hao Tang, and Sharon Goldwater.

CoNLL, 2026. Paper ↗ Code ↗ Poster ↗

We developed a framework for measuring how fully a subspace captures a concept and how well it separates that concept from others. Comparing five estimators in speech and language models, we found that the choice of estimator affects both measures and can therefore shape conclusions about the structure of concept subspaces.

Industry experience

Language Modelling Engineer, Cerence

Jun 2023 – Jul 2024

I improved lexical resources and n-gram language models for deployed in-car hybrid ASR systems, reducing word error rate (WER) by more than 50% relative. I developed a method for aligning tokenisation across the speech recognition and language processing components, which improved entity recognition and intent classification accuracy by more than 10%. I helped pioneer the prototyping of an end-to-end ASR system that outperformed the production system on internal benchmarks and was later deployed.

Applied Scientist Intern, Amazon

Jun 2022 – Nov 2022

I developed a projection module to make synthetic speech features more closely match real speech during training of contextual biasing adapters. With this method, adding synthetic speech to the full real-speech training set reduced named-entity word error rate by 16.7% relative to training on real speech alone.
